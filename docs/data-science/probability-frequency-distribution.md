# Probability Frequency Distribution

A **frequency distribution** shows how often each value or category occurs in a dataset.

## Frequency and relative frequency

- **Frequency** is the number of times a value occurs.
- **Relative frequency** is the fraction of all observations with that value:

$$
\text{relative frequency} = \frac{\text{frequency}}{\text{total number of observations}}
$$

For example, the table summarizes 20 rolls of a six-sided die:

| Outcome | Frequency | Relative frequency |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 2 | 0.10 (10%) |
| 2 | 4 | 0.20 (20%) |
| 3 | 3 | 0.15 (15%) |
| 4 | 5 | 0.25 (25%) |
| 5 | 2 | 0.10 (10%) |
| 6 | 4 | 0.20 (20%) |
| **Total** | **20** | **1.00 (100%)** |

The relative frequency for an outcome is its observed share of the data. For example, outcome 4 occurred in 5 of 20 rolls, so its relative frequency is $5/20 = 0.25$, or 25%.
